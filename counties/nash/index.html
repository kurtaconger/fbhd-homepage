Health Insurance in Nash County, NC for 2027: All 30 Plans Explained

By Kurt Conger, independent licensed NC health insurance agent (NPN 21720013) · Updated November 10, 2026 · 13 minute read · Plan data: HealthCare.gov Marketplace API, 2027 plan year

If you buy your own health insurance in Nash County (Rocky Mount, Nashville, Spring Hope, Middlesex and Bailey) you have one insurer, Blue Cross and Blue Shield of North Carolina, and 30 Marketplace plans for 2027: its 10 designs, each sold in three networks (Blue Home with UNC Health Alliance, Blue Care and Blue Advantage). This guide uses the official HealthCare.gov plan data for Nash County to explain what the plans cost, how the networks differ, and which plan fits which situation.

Key takeaways

  • 30 plans, one insurer, 10 designs. Blue Cross sells each of its 10 designs in three networks with identical benefits: Blue Home with UNC Health Alliance, Blue Care and Blue Advantage.
  • Same benefits, very different prices. Every network sells the federally standardized Bronze, Silver and Gold designs (marked Easy Pricing on HealthCare.gov). At age 45 Blue Home's standardized Silver costs $924 a month; the identical design costs up to $1,161 from Blue Advantage.
  • Your subsidy is set by Blue Home Silver Standard, the second-cheapest Silver plan ($924 at full price, age 45). The credit is the same dollar amount whatever you buy, so a 45-year-old earning $30,000 pays $154 for that plan and $391 for Blue Advantage's standardized Silver.
  • Check the hospital first. In HealthCare.gov's lookup on November 10, 2026, UNC Health Nash was in network on every plan; ECU Health Medical Center, Wilson Medical Center and WakeMed Raleigh and North hospitals were in network on every plan except Blue Home's.
  • Full-price premiums for a 45-year-old run $677 to $917 for Bronze, $917 to $1,179 for Silver and $944 to $1,215 for Gold. A 60-year-old pays about 1.9 times that; a 30-year-old about 0.8 times.
  • No referrals and no catastrophic plan. None of the plans requires a referral to see a specialist, and no catastrophic plan is offered in Nash for 2027.

The Nash County market at a glance

Nash has one Marketplace insurer, Blue Cross NC, which sells its 10 designs in three networks, so the 30 plans are really 10 choices of coverage and three choices of network. That makes three decisions, in order: which network (start with your doctors and hospital), how much coverage, and how your prescriptions are priced. The designs are then described one by one, and a situations table maps common Nash households to the plans worth a close look.

What subsidies do to those numbers

The premium tax credit is a fixed dollar amount based on your income and the price of the second-cheapest Silver plan, which in Nash is Blue Home Silver Standard ($924 a month at age 45). You can spend the credit on any plan. Because the enhanced credits of 2021–2025 have expired, the credit stops entirely at 400% of the federal poverty level ($63,840 for one person, $86,560 for a couple, $132,000 for a family of four), and the Marketplace now recovers any overpaid credit in full at tax time, so your income estimate matters. Two things to notice. First, the credit is the same dollar amount whichever plan you choose, so the full-price gaps between networks pass straight through to what you pay: the standardized Silver has identical benefits everywhere, yet at $30,000 it costs $154 a month from Blue Home and $391 from Blue Advantage. Second, subsidized households get Bronze plans very cheaply: a 45-year-old earning $30,000 pays $0 for 4 of the 12 Bronze plans, including Blue Home Bronze Basic. And as everywhere in North Carolina, a single adult under about $22,000 or a couple under about $29,900 is sent to NC Medicaid instead of receiving a Marketplace credit; the application sorts this out.

Decision 1: Which Blue Cross network?

Blue Cross sells the same 10 designs, with identical benefits, in three networks here, so the choice is about doctors and price. Start with the doctors and the hospital you want to keep.

  • Blue Home with UNC Health Alliance (10 plans, EPO) is built around UNC Health's hospitals and doctors (the UNC Health Alliance). It covers out-of-network care only in emergencies. Children's dental is included. Its standardized plans are the cheapest in the county ($924 for Silver at age 45). UNC Health Nash was in network when we checked; ECU Health Medical Center, Wilson Medical Center and WakeMed Raleigh and North hospitals were not.
  • Blue Care (10 plans, HMO) is Blue Cross NC's statewide network. No referrals; out-of-network care is covered only in emergencies. Children's dental is included. Its standardized Silver costs $1,124 at age 45, $200 a month more than Blue Home's. UNC Health Nash, ECU Health Medical Center, Wilson Medical Center and WakeMed Raleigh and North hospitals were in network when we checked.
  • Blue Advantage (10 plans, PPO) is Blue Cross's nationwide option: it adds the national BlueCard network, so you are in network at Blue Cross doctors in every state. Its out-of-network benefit is thin, so treat it as a nationwide in-network plan. Children's dental is included. Its standardized Silver costs $1,161 at age 45, $237 a month more than Blue Home's. UNC Health Nash, ECU Health Medical Center, Wilson Medical Center and WakeMed Raleigh and North hospitals were in network when we checked.

Choose Blue Home if your doctors and hospital are in its network: the same benefits cost less there than anywhere else in the county. If you want to keep using ECU Health Medical Center, Wilson Medical Center or WakeMed Raleigh and North hospitals, look at Blue Care or Blue Advantage. Choose Blue Advantage if you need in-network care outside North Carolina (months away each year, a student at an out-of-state college, frequent travel); it is the only nationwide network sold here.

Before enrolling, check each doctor and your hospital in the plan's own directory (Blue Home with UNC Health Alliance, Blue Care, Blue Advantage), or add them in our Covanu tool, which checks them against every plan's network. In Nash County, UNC Health Nash in Rocky Mount is the local hospital; residents also use ECU Health Medical Center in Greenville, Wilson Medical Center and WakeMed in Raleigh.

Decision 2: How much coverage do you expect to use?

Bronze, Silver and Gold describe how costs are split between you and the plan: roughly 60%, 70% and 80% paid by the plan for an average person. In Nash the practical differences are these:

  • Bronze (12 plans, $677 to $917 at 45): deductibles up to $8,700, so you pay for most care yourself until something serious happens. All 12 Bronze plans are HSA-eligible for 2027 under the 2025 federal tax law, which lets you set aside up to $4,500 (single) or $9,000 (family) pre-tax, plus $1,000 if you are 55 or older.
  • Silver (9 plans, $917 to $1,179): the plans to look at first if you qualify for a subsidy, because incomes under about $39,900 (single) or $82,500 (family of four) unlock cost-sharing reductions that only apply to Silver plans. At $30,000 for one person the standardized Silver's deductible drops from $6,400 to $800 and its out-of-pocket maximum from $10,300 to $3,600. Without a cost-sharing reduction, Silver is a middle ground that often loses to either Bronze or Gold on total cost.
  • Gold (9 plans, $944 to $1,215): $0 to $2,500 deductibles and flat copays for most care. The cheapest Gold, Blue Home Gold Premier A, costs $267 a month more than the cheapest Bronze at age 45, which is worth it if you take brand-name or specialty drugs, see specialists regularly, or are planning surgery or a pregnancy.

Decision 3: How are your prescriptions priced?

Blue Cross uses two drug lists, a 4-tier list on its Standard plans and a 5-tier list on the rest, and the finding from our other county guides holds here: they covered the same 48 common drugs. Our Covanu tool checks a list of medications against every plan at once. Beyond coverage, the plan design decides what you pay:

  • On the standardized plans, preferred brand drugs are flat copays with no deductible on Silver ($40) and Gold ($30), and specialty drugs are $350 after the deductible on Silver or $250 with no deductible on Gold.
  • Most other designs charge 25% to 50% coinsurance for non-preferred and specialty drugs, usually after a deductible, and several put brand drugs behind the deductible or a separate drug deductible. Someone on an expensive brand drug can pay thousands before the plan contributes.

The arithmetic for brand and specialty drugs is in Bronze vs. Silver when you take medications. The short version: one brand-name prescription is often enough to make a standardized Silver cheaper than Bronze over a year, and a specialty drug makes a standardized Gold the cheapest plan on the shelf.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Blue Cross the only insurer in Nash County?

For 2027, Blue Cross and Blue Shield of North Carolina is the only carrier offering individual Marketplace plans in Nash County. The other insurers that sell Marketplace plans elsewhere in North Carolina (Ambetter, AmeriHealth Caritas Next, Oscar and UnitedHealthcare) do not include Nash in their 2027 service areas. Blue Cross sells its 10 designs in Blue Home, Blue Care and Blue Advantage, for 30 plans.

Is UNC Health Nash in network?

When we checked HealthCare.gov's doctor and hospital lookup on November 10, 2026, UNC Health Nash was in network on every plan; ECU Health Medical Center, Wilson Medical Center and WakeMed Raleigh and North hospitals were in network on every plan except Blue Home's. Networks change, and a hospital being in network does not mean every doctor who works there is, so check the specific doctors and facilities you use before you enroll.

Why do plans with the same benefits cost so differently after the subsidy?

Because the tax credit is a fixed dollar amount based on the second-cheapest Silver plan in your county, which in Nash is Blue Home Silver Standard ($924 a month at age 45). The credit makes that plan cost the same share of your income wherever you live; any plan that costs more at full price costs exactly that much more after the credit. That is why the standardized Silver, identical in benefits in every network, costs a 45-year-old earning $30,000 $154 a month from Blue Home and $391 from Blue Advantage.

What are the standardized "Easy Pricing" plans?

Plans whose deductibles, copays and coinsurance are set by the federal government, so a standardized plan at a given metal level has the same benefits whichever insurer sells it. HealthCare.gov labels them Easy Pricing. In Nash they are Blue Cross NC's Standard (Gold Standard A for Gold) plans. Because the benefits match, the comparison comes down to price, network and extras such as children's dental: at age 45 the standardized Silver ranges from $924 (Blue Home) to $1,161 (Blue Advantage).

Do I need a referral to see a specialist?

No. None of the plans sold here requires a referral from a primary care doctor to see a specialist, including the HMOs.

Which plans work outside North Carolina?

Only Blue Advantage, Blue Cross's PPO, gives you in-network access to doctors nationwide, through the BlueCard network. It costs about 3% more than the same Blue Care design: $27 to $40 a month at age 45, $51 to $76 at 60. The other plans are built around North Carolina providers. Every plan covers emergency care anywhere.

Is there a catastrophic plan in Nash County?

No. For 2027 no catastrophic plan is offered in Nash County, even for people under 30. The Bronze plans are the lowest-premium options, starting at $492 a month at age 27 before any subsidy.

Do the plans include dental and vision?

Children's dental is included in every Blue Home, Blue Care and Blue Advantage plan. Included children's dental is worth about $250 to $700 a year per child, what a stand-alone children's dental plan costs in North Carolina for 2027 ($21 to $58 a month). It pays for checkups in full; on most plans, fillings and other treatment count toward the medical deductible, while a stand-alone plan has a $25 to $155 deductible and caps a child's costs at $350 to $450 a year, so a child who needs fillings can come out ahead with one. Adult dental and vision are not included in any plan sold here; buy them separately if you want them.

What happens if my income is under about $22,000?

North Carolina expanded Medicaid in December 2023. A single adult with income under roughly 138% of the federal poverty level (about $22,000 for one person, $29,900 for a couple, $45,500 for a family of four in 2027) is directed to NC Medicaid rather than receiving a Marketplace subsidy. The Marketplace application checks this automatically.

When can I enroll?

Open Enrollment for 2027 coverage runs November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027. Enroll by December 15 for coverage starting January 1. Outside Open Enrollment you need a qualifying event, such as losing other coverage, moving, marriage or a new baby.
