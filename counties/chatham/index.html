Health Insurance in Chatham County, NC for 2027: All 10 Plans Explained

By Kurt Conger, independent licensed NC health insurance agent (NPN 21720013) · Updated November 10, 2026 · 11 minute read · Plan data: HealthCare.gov Marketplace API, 2027 plan year

If you buy your own health insurance in Chatham County (Pittsboro, Siler City, Goldston, Bennett and Fearrington Village) you have one insurer, Blue Cross and Blue Shield of North Carolina, and 10 Marketplace plans for 2027, all in one network: Blue Home with UNC Health Alliance, an EPO built around UNC Health. This guide uses the official HealthCare.gov plan data for Chatham County to explain what the plans cost, how the plans differ, and which plan fits which situation.

Key takeaways

  • 10 plans, one insurer, one network. Every plan uses Blue Cross's Blue Home with UNC Health Alliance network, an EPO built around UNC Health; the choice is how much coverage you want.
  • Check the hospital first. In HealthCare.gov's lookup on November 10, 2026, UNC Health Chatham and UNC Medical Center were in network; Duke University Hospital and WakeMed Cary Hospital were not in network.
  • Full-price premiums for a 45-year-old run $573 to $618 for Bronze, $776 to $793 for Silver and $799 to $817 for Gold. A 60-year-old pays about 1.9 times that; a 30-year-old about 0.8 times.
  • No nationwide network. Every plan sold in Chatham is built around North Carolina providers and covers only emergencies out of state.
  • No referrals and no catastrophic plan. None of the plans requires a referral to see a specialist, and no catastrophic plan is offered in Chatham for 2027.

What subsidies do to those numbers

The premium tax credit is a fixed dollar amount based on your income and the price of the second-cheapest Silver plan, which in Chatham is Blue Home Silver Standard ($782 a month at age 45). You can spend the credit on any plan. Because the enhanced credits of 2021–2025 have expired, the credit stops entirely at 400% of the federal poverty level ($63,840 for one person, $86,560 for a couple, $132,000 for a family of four), and the Marketplace now recovers any overpaid credit in full at tax time, so your income estimate matters. The network: Blue Home with UNC Health Alliance

Every plan in Chatham uses one network, so the network decision is made for you. Before you enroll, make sure the doctors and hospital you use are in it.

  • Blue Home with UNC Health Alliance (10 plans, EPO) is built around UNC Health's hospitals and doctors (the UNC Health Alliance). It covers out-of-network care only in emergencies. Children's dental is included. UNC Health Chatham and UNC Medical Center were in network when we checked; Duke University Hospital and WakeMed Cary Hospital were not.

No plan sold in Chatham has a nationwide network, so if you spend months out of state, routine care there will not be covered; every plan still covers emergencies anywhere.

Before enrolling, check each doctor and your hospital in the plan's own directory (Blue Home with UNC Health Alliance), or add them in our Covanu tool, which checks them against every plan's network. In Chatham County, UNC Health Chatham in Siler City is the local hospital; residents also use UNC Medical Center in Chapel Hill, Duke University Hospital and, in the northeast of the county, WakeMed Cary.

Decision 2: How much coverage do you expect to use?

Bronze, Silver and Gold describe how costs are split between you and the plan: roughly 60%, 70% and 80% paid by the plan for an average person. In Chatham the practical differences are these:

  • Bronze (4 plans, $573 to $618 at 45): deductibles up to $8,700, so you pay for most care yourself until something serious happens. All 4 Bronze plans are HSA-eligible for 2027 under the 2025 federal tax law, which lets you set aside up to $4,500 (single) or $9,000 (family) pre-tax, plus $1,000 if you are 55 or older.
  • Silver (3 plans, $776 to $793): the plans to look at first if you qualify for a subsidy, because incomes under about $39,900 (single) or $82,500 (family of four) unlock cost-sharing reductions that only apply to Silver plans. At $30,000 for one person the standardized Silver's deductible drops from $6,400 to $800 and its out-of-pocket maximum from $10,300 to $3,600. Without a cost-sharing reduction, Silver is a middle ground that often loses to either Bronze or Gold on total cost.
  • Gold (3 plans, $799 to $817): $0 to $2,500 deductibles and flat copays for most care. The cheapest Gold, Blue Home Gold Premier A, costs $226 a month more than the cheapest Bronze at age 45, which is worth it if you take brand-name or specialty drugs, see specialists regularly, or are planning surgery or a pregnancy.

Decision 3: How are your prescriptions priced?

Blue Cross uses two drug lists, a 4-tier list on its Standard plans and a 5-tier list on the rest, and the finding from our other county guides holds here: they covered the same 48 common drugs. Our Covanu tool checks a list of medications against every plan at once. Beyond coverage, the plan design decides what you pay:

  • On the standardized plans, preferred brand drugs are flat copays with no deductible on Silver ($40) and Gold ($30), and specialty drugs are $350 after the deductible on Silver or $250 with no deductible on Gold.
  • Most other designs charge 25% to 50% coinsurance for non-preferred and specialty drugs, usually after a deductible, and several put brand drugs behind the deductible or a separate drug deductible. Someone on an expensive brand drug can pay thousands before the plan contributes.

The arithmetic for brand and specialty drugs is in Bronze vs. Silver when you take medications. The short version: one brand-name prescription is often enough to make a standardized Silver cheaper than Bronze over a year, and a specialty drug makes a standardized Gold the cheapest plan on the shelf.

$7,000 deductible · $12,000 out-of-pocket max · HSA-eligible

Three primary care visits a year are free, then $95; specialists are $175; preferred generics are $25. Everything else, including brand drugs, imaging, the emergency room and hospital stays, is 50% coinsurance after the $7,000 deductible. It is the cheapest Bronze plan in the county.

Bronze Standard $588Best for: Bronze buyers who want copays rather than coinsurance on a Blue Cross network

$7,500 deductible · $12,000 out-of-pocket max · HSA-eligible · standardized design

The federally standardized Bronze: $50 primary care and $100 specialist copays from day one, $25 generics, and after the deductible flat copays of $50 (preferred brand), $100 (non-preferred) and $500 (specialty). Children's dental is included.

Bronze Complete $597Best for: Bronze buyers with one or two brand-name prescriptions

$4,000 medical deductible plus a separate $1,000 drug deductible · $12,000 out-of-pocket max · HSA-eligible

The lowest medical deductible among Blue Cross's Bronze plans. Primary care is $60, specialists $120, generics $20. Brand drugs become $75 (preferred) or $150 (non-preferred) copays once you have met the separate $1,000 drug deductible. Hospital care is 50% after the $4,000 medical deductible.

Bronze HSA Eligible Plus $618Best for: disciplined HSA savers who want the lowest worst-case cost on a Bronze plan

$8,700 deductible · $8,700 out-of-pocket max · HSA-eligible

A true high-deductible plan: you pay everything except preventive care until you reach $8,700, and then the plan pays 100%. There are no copays to track, and the ceiling is $3,300 lower than the $12,000 maximum on most Bronze plans.

Silver Preferred $776Best for: subsidized households who want a Blue Cross network and take mostly generics

$2,700 deductible · $12,000 out-of-pocket max · with cost-sharing reductions at $30,000 income: $325 deductible, $4,000 max

Three free primary care visits then $35, specialists $75, generics $15. Every brand and specialty drug is 50% coinsurance after the deductible, so it suits generic users; with cost-sharing reductions its deductible is among the lowest of any plan. It is the cheapest Silver plan in the county.

Silver Standard $782Best for: anyone on a preferred brand-name drug who wants a Blue Cross network

$6,400 deductible · $10,300 out-of-pocket max · standardized design · with cost-sharing reductions at $30,000 income: $800 deductible, $3,600 max

The federally standardized Silver: $40 primary care, $80 specialist, $20 generic and $40 preferred brand copays from day one; non-preferred brands ($80) and specialty drugs ($350) after the deductible, and 40% for hospital care after it. In Chatham this is the benchmark plan: your tax credit is calculated from its price.

Silver Choice A $793Best for: Blue Cross members who want a lower medical deductible than Silver Standard and take at most one or two brand drugs

$3,500 medical deductible plus a separate $200 drug deductible · $12,000 out-of-pocket max · with cost-sharing reductions at $30,000 income: $550 deductible, $4,000 max

Three free primary care visits then $45, specialists $95, generics $15, and brand drugs at $35 (preferred) or $70 (non-preferred) copays after a small $200 drug deductible. Specialty drugs and hospital care are 50% after the deductibles.

Gold Premier A $799Best for: frequent doctor visits and brand-name (not specialty) prescriptions on a Blue Cross network

$1,800 medical deductible plus a separate $400 drug deductible · $12,000 out-of-pocket max

Three free primary care visits then $15, specialists $45, generics $10, brand drugs $35 or $60 after the $400 drug deductible. Hospital and emergency care are 30% after the $1,800 deductible, and specialty drugs are 50% after the deductible: strong for doctor visits and ordinary prescriptions, weak for specialty medications. It is the cheapest Gold plan in the county.

Gold Standard A $806Best for: anyone on a specialty drug who wants a Blue Cross network

$2,500 deductible · $8,600 out-of-pocket max · standardized design

The federally standardized Gold: primary care $30, specialists $60, generics $15, preferred brand $30, non-preferred $60 and specialty $250, all copays with no deductible; hospital care is 25% after the deductible.

Gold All Copay A $817Best for: people who want to know the price of every visit in advance, and those expecting a hospital stay

$0 deductible · $12,000 out-of-pocket max

No deductible at all. Every service is a fixed copay: $30 primary care, $60 specialist, $10 generic, $60 preferred brand, $120 non-preferred, $220 specialty, $1,500 emergency room and $2,500 per hospital stay. You give up the lower out-of-pocket maximum of Gold Standard A in exchange for never having to meet a deductible.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Blue Cross the only insurer in Chatham County?

For 2027, Blue Cross and Blue Shield of North Carolina is the only carrier offering individual Marketplace plans in Chatham County. The other insurers that sell Marketplace plans elsewhere in North Carolina (Ambetter, AmeriHealth Caritas Next, Oscar and UnitedHealthcare) do not include Chatham in their 2027 service areas. Blue Cross sells its 10 designs in one network here, Blue Home with UNC Health Alliance.

Is UNC Health Chatham in network?

When we checked HealthCare.gov's doctor and hospital lookup on November 10, 2026, UNC Health Chatham and UNC Medical Center were in network; Duke University Hospital and WakeMed Cary Hospital were not in network. Networks change, and a hospital being in network does not mean every doctor who works there is, so check the specific doctors and facilities you use before you enroll.

Do I need a referral to see a specialist?

No. None of the plans sold here requires a referral from a primary care doctor to see a specialist.

Which plans work outside North Carolina?

None of the plans sold in Chatham has a nationwide network: Blue Cross's Blue Advantage PPO, which uses the national BlueCard network, is not sold here, and every plan is an EPO built around North Carolina providers. Every plan covers emergency care anywhere, but routine care out of state is not covered. A college student out of state may be better served by the school's student health plan.

Is there a catastrophic plan in Chatham County?

No. For 2027 no catastrophic plan is offered in Chatham County, even for people under 30. The Bronze plans are the lowest-premium options, starting at $416 a month at age 27 before any subsidy.

Do the plans include dental and vision?

Children's dental is included in every Blue Home plan. Included children's dental is worth about $250 to $700 a year per child, what a stand-alone children's dental plan costs in North Carolina for 2027 ($21 to $58 a month). It pays for checkups in full; on most plans, fillings and other treatment count toward the medical deductible, while a stand-alone plan has a $25 to $155 deductible and caps a child's costs at $350 to $450 a year, so a child who needs fillings can come out ahead with one. Adult dental and vision are not included in any plan sold here; buy them separately if you want them.

What happens if my income is under about $22,000?

North Carolina expanded Medicaid in December 2023. A single adult with income under roughly 138% of the federal poverty level (about $22,000 for one person, $29,900 for a couple, $45,500 for a family of four in 2027) is directed to NC Medicaid rather than receiving a Marketplace subsidy. The Marketplace application checks this automatically.

When can I enroll?

Open Enrollment for 2027 coverage runs November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027. Enroll by December 15 for coverage starting January 1. Outside Open Enrollment you need a qualifying event, such as losing other coverage, moving, marriage or a new baby.
