Health Insurance in Robeson County, NC for 2027: All 54 Plans Explained

By Kurt Conger, independent licensed NC health insurance agent (NPN 21720013) · Updated November 10, 2026 · 21 minute read · Plan data: HealthCare.gov Marketplace API, 2027 plan year

If you buy your own health insurance in Robeson County (Lumberton, Pembroke, Red Springs, St. Pauls, Fairmont, Maxton and Rowland) you have three insurers and 54 Marketplace plans to choose from for 2027: Ambetter of North Carolina, Blue Cross and Blue Shield of North Carolina and UnitedHealthcare. This guide uses the official HealthCare.gov plan data for Robeson County to explain what the plans cost, how the insurers and their networks differ, and which plan fits which situation.

Key takeaways

  • 54 plans, three insurers, 29 designs. Ambetter 19, Blue Cross NC 20 (10 designs in two networks) and UnitedHealthcare 15. Many are one design sold twice, with and without adult dental and vision.
  • Same benefits, very different prices. Every network sells the federally standardized Bronze, Silver and Gold designs (marked Easy Pricing on HealthCare.gov). At age 45 Ambetter's standardized Silver costs $711 a month; the identical design costs up to $1,172 from Blue Advantage.
  • Your subsidy is set by Ambetter Clear Silver with $0 Insulin Options, the second-cheapest Silver plan ($704 at full price, age 45). The credit is the same dollar amount whatever you buy, so a 45-year-old earning $30,000 pays $154 for that plan and $622 for Blue Advantage's standardized Silver.
  • Check the hospital first. In HealthCare.gov's lookup on November 10, 2026, UNC Health Southeastern was in network on every plan except Ambetter's; Cape Fear Valley Medical Center was in network on every plan; Scotland Memorial Hospital was in network on Blue Care and Blue Advantage plans and not on Ambetter or UnitedHealthcare plans.
  • Full-price premiums for a 45-year-old run $616 to $926 for Bronze, $698 to $1,190 for Silver and $785 to $1,296 for Gold. A 60-year-old pays about 1.9 times that; a 30-year-old about 0.8 times.
  • No referrals and no catastrophic plan. None of the plans requires a referral to see a specialist, and no catastrophic plan is offered in Robeson for 2027.

The Robeson County market at a glance

Robeson has three Marketplace insurers, but the number of plans overstates how hard the choice is. The 54 plans are 29 designs, many of them sold more than once, and they come in four networks. That makes three decisions, in order: which insurer and network (start with your doctors and hospital), how much coverage, and how your prescriptions are priced. The designs are then described insurer by insurer, and a situations table maps common Robeson households to the plans worth a close look.

What subsidies do to those numbers

The premium tax credit is a fixed dollar amount based on your income and the price of the second-cheapest Silver plan, which in Robeson is Ambetter Clear Silver with $0 Insulin Options ($704 a month at age 45). You can spend the credit on any plan. Because the enhanced credits of 2021–2025 have expired, the credit stops entirely at 400% of the federal poverty level ($63,840 for one person, $86,560 for a couple, $132,000 for a family of four), and the Marketplace now recovers any overpaid credit in full at tax time, so your income estimate matters. Three things to notice. First, the credit is the same dollar amount whichever plan you choose, so the full-price gaps between insurers pass straight through to what you pay: the standardized Silver has identical benefits everywhere, yet at $30,000 it costs $161 a month from Ambetter and $622 from Blue Advantage. Second, no plan here is free at $30,000. At age 45 the cheapest Bronze costs only $88 a month less than the benchmark, so the credit does not cover it and a 45-year-old earning $30,000 still pays $66. Third, families should mind children's dental: Ambetter plans leave it out, while Blue Care, Blue Advantage and UnitedHealthcare plans include it, a difference worth about $250 to $700 a year per child, the cost of a stand-alone children's dental plan. And as everywhere in North Carolina, a single adult under about $22,000 or a couple under about $29,900 is sent to NC Medicaid instead of receiving a Marketplace credit; the application sorts this out.

Decision 1: Which insurer and network?

Where Blue Cross is the only insurer, this decision is between two versions of the same plan. In Robeson it is the decision that matters most, because the networks really differ and the prices differ far more than the benefits do. Start with the doctors and the hospital you want to keep.

  • Ambetter (19 plans, HMO) is Ambetter of North Carolina's network. As an HMO it covers out-of-network care only in emergencies, but no referrals are needed for specialists. Children's dental is not included. Members get a free YMCA membership at participating North Carolina Ys, the Ambetter Perks discount program and up to $500 in My Health Pays rewards for healthy activities. Its standardized plans are the cheapest in the county ($711 for Silver at age 45). Cape Fear Valley Medical Center was in network when we checked; UNC Health Southeastern and Scotland Memorial Hospital were not.
  • UnitedHealthcare (15 plans, HMO) is UnitedHealthcare's network, with $0 insulin on every plan, $0 virtual urgent care on most, and children's dental included. Out-of-network care is covered only in emergencies. Its standardized Silver costs $1,079 at age 45, $368 a month more than Ambetter's. UNC Health Southeastern and Cape Fear Valley Medical Center were in network when we checked; Scotland Memorial Hospital was not.
  • Blue Care (10 plans, HMO) is Blue Cross NC's statewide network. No referrals; out-of-network care is covered only in emergencies. Children's dental is included. Its standardized Silver costs $1,141 at age 45, $430 a month more than Ambetter's. UNC Health Southeastern, Cape Fear Valley Medical Center and Scotland Memorial Hospital were in network when we checked.
  • Blue Advantage (10 plans, PPO) is Blue Cross's nationwide option: it adds the national BlueCard network, so you are in network at Blue Cross doctors in every state. Its out-of-network benefit is thin, so treat it as a nationwide in-network plan. Children's dental is included. Its standardized Silver costs $1,172 at age 45, $461 a month more than Ambetter's. UNC Health Southeastern, Cape Fear Valley Medical Center and Scotland Memorial Hospital were in network when we checked.

Choose Ambetter if your doctors and hospital are in its network: the same benefits cost less there than anywhere else in the county. If you want to keep using UNC Health Southeastern, look at Blue Care, Blue Advantage or UnitedHealthcare. If you want to keep using Scotland Memorial Hospital, look at Blue Care or Blue Advantage. Choose Blue Advantage if you need in-network care outside North Carolina (months away each year, a student at an out-of-state college, frequent travel); it is the only nationwide network sold here. If you use insulin, weigh UnitedHealthcare's $0 insulin against its higher premiums before choosing it; Decision 3 has the numbers.

Before enrolling, check each doctor and your hospital in the plan's own directory (Ambetter, Blue Care, Blue Advantage, UnitedHealthcare), or add them in our Covanu tool, which checks them against every plan's network. In Robeson County, UNC Health Southeastern in Lumberton is the local hospital, with Cape Fear Valley Medical Center in Fayetteville and Scotland Memorial Hospital in Laurinburg nearby.

Decision 2: How much coverage do you expect to use?

Bronze, Silver and Gold describe how costs are split between you and the plan: roughly 60%, 70% and 80% paid by the plan for an average person. In Robeson the practical differences are these:

  • Bronze (19 plans, $616 to $926 at 45): deductibles up to $11,750, so you pay for most care yourself until something serious happens, although two Bronze designs have no medical deductible (copays or coinsurance apply from the first dollar instead). All 19 Bronze plans are HSA-eligible for 2027 under the 2025 federal tax law, which lets you set aside up to $4,500 (single) or $9,000 (family) pre-tax, plus $1,000 if you are 55 or older.
  • Silver (20 plans, $698 to $1,190): the plans to look at first if you qualify for a subsidy, because incomes under about $39,900 (single) or $82,500 (family of four) unlock cost-sharing reductions that only apply to Silver plans. At $30,000 for one person the standardized Silver's deductible drops from $6,400 to $800 and its out-of-pocket maximum from $10,300 to $3,600. Without a cost-sharing reduction, Silver is a middle ground that often loses to either Bronze or Gold on total cost.
  • Gold (15 plans, $785 to $1,296): $0 to $2,500 deductibles and flat copays for most care. The cheapest Gold, Ambetter Signature Gold, costs $169 a month more than the cheapest Bronze at age 45, which is worth it if you take brand-name or specialty drugs, see specialists regularly, or are planning surgery or a pregnancy.

Decision 3: How are your prescriptions priced?

Each insurer has its own drug list, so check every prescription against the plans you are considering; our Covanu tool checks a list of medications against every plan in the county at once. (For Blue Cross, the finding from our other county guides holds here: its two drug lists covered the same 48 common drugs.) Beyond coverage, the plan design decides what you pay:

  • On the standardized plans, preferred brand drugs are flat copays with no deductible on Silver ($40) and Gold ($30), and specialty drugs are $350 after the deductible on Silver or $250 with no deductible on Gold.
  • Most other designs charge 25% to 50% coinsurance for non-preferred and specialty drugs, usually after a deductible, and several put brand drugs behind the deductible or a separate drug deductible. Someone on an expensive brand drug can pay thousands before the plan contributes.
  • Insulin and condition-specific plans. UnitedHealthcare includes $0 insulin on every plan; Ambetter's Clear Silver has $0 insulin options, its Enhanced CHF Heart Care Silver has $0 options for certain heart-failure drugs, and its Enhanced Asthma/COPD Care Silver makes listed asthma and COPD drugs and pulmonologist visits free. In the official coverage example for a year of managing type 2 diabetes, the estimated cost to you on the standardized Bronze is $4,720 with Ambetter, $4,620 with Blue Cross NC and $700 with UnitedHealthcare: same deductible and copays, different drug lists and insulin terms. The example prices insulin at the plan's own rate, before any manufacturer program.
  • What insulin costs without a $0 benefit. It is priced like any brand-name drug: a preferred brand is a $40 copay with no deductible on the standardized Silver and $30 on the standardized Gold, while on most Bronze plans you pay the pharmacy price, about $66 to $72 for a vial of Humalog or NovoLog since the 2023–2024 list-price cuts, until you meet the deductible. Lilly, Novo Nordisk and Sanofi each cap their insulins at $35 a month for people with commercial coverage, which includes Marketplace plans, so a two-insulin routine usually costs $35 to $80 a month without a $0 benefit, about $400 to $1,000 a year. That is roughly what $0 insulin is worth to someone who uses those programs. Here UnitedHealthcare's standardized Silver costs $368 a month more than Ambetter's identical plan at age 45, $4,416 a year, so for most insulin users a cheaper plan plus a maker's $35 program costs less over the year. Ambetter's Clear Silver is the benchmark Silver your credit is based on, so its $0 insulin options carry no extra premium; check that your insulin is on its $0 list, and weigh its $7,900 deductible ($2,450 with cost-sharing reductions at $30,000, single).

The arithmetic for brand and specialty drugs is in Bronze vs. Silver when you take medications. The short version: one brand-name prescription is often enough to make a standardized Silver cheaper than Bronze over a year, and a specialty drug makes a standardized Gold the cheapest plan on the shelf.

Frequently asked questions

Which insurers sell Marketplace plans in Robeson County for 2027?

Three: Ambetter of North Carolina (19 plans), Blue Cross and Blue Shield of North Carolina (20 plans) and UnitedHealthcare (15 plans), for 54 plans in all. Blue Cross sells each of its designs in Blue Care and Blue Advantage, and Ambetter and UnitedHealthcare sell some designs both with and without adult dental and vision, so the 54 plans come down to 29 distinct designs.

Is UNC Health Southeastern in network?

When we checked HealthCare.gov's doctor and hospital lookup on November 10, 2026, UNC Health Southeastern was in network on every plan except Ambetter's; Cape Fear Valley Medical Center was in network on every plan; Scotland Memorial Hospital was in network on Blue Care and Blue Advantage plans and not on Ambetter or UnitedHealthcare plans. Networks change, and a hospital being in network does not mean every doctor who works there is, so check the specific doctors and facilities you use before you enroll.

Why do plans with the same benefits cost so differently after the subsidy?

Because the tax credit is a fixed dollar amount based on the second-cheapest Silver plan in your county, which in Robeson is Ambetter Clear Silver with $0 Insulin Options ($704 a month at age 45). The credit makes that plan cost the same share of your income wherever you live; any plan that costs more at full price costs exactly that much more after the credit. That is why the standardized Silver, identical in benefits from every insurer, costs a 45-year-old earning $30,000 $161 a month from Ambetter and $622 from Blue Advantage.

What are the standardized "Easy Pricing" plans?

Plans whose deductibles, copays and coinsurance are set by the federal government, so a standardized plan at a given metal level has the same benefits whichever insurer sells it. HealthCare.gov labels them Easy Pricing. In Robeson they are Ambetter's Signature plans, Blue Cross NC's Standard (Gold Standard A for Gold) plans and UnitedHealthcare's Standard plans. Because the benefits match, the comparison comes down to price, network and extras such as children's dental: at age 45 the standardized Silver ranges from $711 (Ambetter) to $1,172 (Blue Advantage).

Do I need a referral to see a specialist?

No. None of the plans sold here requires a referral from a primary care doctor to see a specialist, including the HMOs.

Which plans work outside North Carolina?

Only Blue Advantage, Blue Cross's PPO, gives you in-network access to doctors nationwide, through the BlueCard network. It costs about 3% more than the same Blue Care design: $24 to $35 a month at age 45, $44 to $65 at 60. The other plans are built around North Carolina providers. Every plan covers emergency care anywhere.

Is there a catastrophic plan in Robeson County?

No. For 2027 no catastrophic plan is offered in Robeson County, even for people under 30. The Bronze plans are the lowest-premium options, starting at $447 a month at age 27 before any subsidy.

Do the plans include dental and vision?

Children's dental is included in every Blue Care, Blue Advantage and UnitedHealthcare plan, but not in Ambetter plans; families choosing those should add a stand-alone dental plan for the children. Included children's dental is worth about $250 to $700 a year per child, what a stand-alone children's dental plan costs in North Carolina for 2027 ($21 to $58 a month). It pays for checkups in full; on most plans, fillings and other treatment count toward the medical deductible, while a stand-alone plan has a $25 to $155 deductible and caps a child's costs at $350 to $450 a year, so a child who needs fillings can come out ahead with one. Adult dental and vision come only with the versions that say so, from Ambetter (about $18 to $25 a month more at age 45) and UnitedHealthcare (about $23 to $24 a month more at age 45).

What happens if my income is under about $22,000?

North Carolina expanded Medicaid in December 2023. A single adult with income under roughly 138% of the federal poverty level (about $22,000 for one person, $29,900 for a couple, $45,500 for a family of four in 2027) is directed to NC Medicaid rather than receiving a Marketplace subsidy. The Marketplace application checks this automatically.

When can I enroll?

Open Enrollment for 2027 coverage runs November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027. Enroll by December 15 for coverage starting January 1. Outside Open Enrollment you need a qualifying event, such as losing other coverage, moving, marriage or a new baby.
