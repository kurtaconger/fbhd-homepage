Health Insurance in Lincoln County, NC for 2027: All 72 Plans Explained

By Kurt Conger, independent licensed NC health insurance agent (NPN 21720013) · Updated November 10, 2026 · 22 minute read · Plan data: HealthCare.gov Marketplace API, 2027 plan year

If you buy your own health insurance in Lincoln County (Lincolnton, Denver, Iron Station and Crouse) you have three insurers and 72 Marketplace plans to choose from for 2027: Ambetter of North Carolina, Blue Cross and Blue Shield of North Carolina and Oscar. This guide uses the official HealthCare.gov plan data for Lincoln County to explain what the plans cost, how the insurers and their networks differ, and which plan fits which situation.

Key takeaways

  • 72 plans, three insurers, 32 designs. Ambetter 15, Blue Cross NC 40 (10 designs in four networks) and Oscar 17. Many are one design sold twice, with and without adult dental and vision.
  • Same benefits, very different prices. Every network sells the federally standardized Bronze, Silver and Gold designs (marked Easy Pricing on HealthCare.gov). At age 45 Ambetter's standardized Silver costs $743 a month; the identical design costs up to $1,241 from Blue Advantage.
  • Your subsidy is set by Ambetter Signature Silver, the second-cheapest Silver plan ($743 at full price, age 45). The credit is the same dollar amount whatever you buy, so a 45-year-old earning $30,000 pays $154 for that plan and $652 for Blue Advantage's standardized Silver.
  • Check the hospital first. In HealthCare.gov's lookup on November 10, 2026, Atrium Health Lincoln was in network on every plan except Blue Value's; CaroMont Regional Medical Center was in network on every plan except Ambetter's and Blue Local's; Catawba Valley Medical Center was in network on Blue Value, Blue Care and Blue Advantage plans and not on Ambetter, Blue Local or Oscar plans.
  • Full-price premiums for a 45-year-old run $644 to $981 for Bronze, $727 to $1,260 for Silver and $830 to $1,299 for Gold. A 60-year-old pays about 1.9 times that; a 30-year-old about 0.8 times.
  • No referrals and no catastrophic plan. None of the plans requires a referral to see a specialist, and no catastrophic plan is offered in Lincoln for 2027.

The Lincoln County market at a glance

Lincoln has three Marketplace insurers, but the number of plans overstates how hard the choice is. The 72 plans are 32 designs, many of them sold more than once, and they come in six networks. That makes three decisions, in order: which insurer and network (start with your doctors and hospital), how much coverage, and how your prescriptions are priced. The designs are then described insurer by insurer, and a situations table maps common Lincoln households to the plans worth a close look.

What subsidies do to those numbers

The premium tax credit is a fixed dollar amount based on your income and the price of the second-cheapest Silver plan, which in Lincoln is Ambetter Signature Silver ($743 a month at age 45). You can spend the credit on any plan. Because the enhanced credits of 2021–2025 have expired, the credit stops entirely at 400% of the federal poverty level ($63,840 for one person, $86,560 for a couple, $132,000 for a family of four), and the Marketplace now recovers any overpaid credit in full at tax time, so your income estimate matters. At $30,000 for one person, the deductible and out-of-pocket maximum drop to $850 and $3,700 on Ambetter Streamlined Silver, $800 and $3,600 on Ambetter Signature Silver and $800 and $3,600 on Blue Value Silver Standard; at $35,000 (age 27) they drop to $3,450 and $8,500 on Ambetter Streamlined Silver, $2,900 and $9,000 on Ambetter Signature Silver and $2,900 and $9,000 on Blue Value Silver Standard.

Three things to notice. First, the credit is the same dollar amount whichever plan you choose, so the full-price gaps between insurers pass straight through to what you pay: the standardized Silver has identical benefits everywhere, yet at $30,000 it costs $154 a month from Ambetter and $652 from Blue Advantage. Second, no plan here is free at $30,000. At age 45 the cheapest Bronze costs only $99 a month less than the benchmark, so the credit does not cover it and a 45-year-old earning $30,000 still pays $55. Third, families should mind children's dental: Ambetter and Oscar plans leave it out, while Blue Value, Blue Local, Blue Care and Blue Advantage plans include it, a difference worth about $250 to $700 a year per child, the cost of a stand-alone children's dental plan. And as everywhere in North Carolina, a single adult under about $22,000 or a couple under about $29,900 is sent to NC Medicaid instead of receiving a Marketplace credit; the application sorts this out.

Decision 1: Which insurer and network?

Where Blue Cross is the only insurer, this decision is between two versions of the same plan. In Lincoln it is the decision that matters most, because the networks really differ and the prices differ far more than the benefits do. Start with the doctors and the hospital you want to keep.

  • Ambetter with Atrium Health (15 plans, HMO) is Ambetter of North Carolina's network built with Atrium Health, whose hospitals and doctors anchor it. As an HMO it covers out-of-network care only in emergencies, but no referrals are needed for specialists. Children's dental is not included. Members get a free YMCA membership at participating North Carolina Ys, the Ambetter Perks discount program and up to $500 in My Health Pays rewards for healthy activities. Its standardized plans are the cheapest in the county ($743 for Silver at age 45). Atrium Health Lincoln was in network when we checked; CaroMont Regional Medical Center and Catawba Valley Medical Center were not.
  • Oscar with Atrium Health (17 plans, HMO) is Oscar's network built around Atrium Health's hospitals and doctors, with $0 virtual urgent care on every plan and a Care Team in the Oscar app. Out-of-network care is covered only in emergencies, and children's dental is not included. Its standardized Silver costs $822 at age 45, $79 a month more than Ambetter's. Atrium Health Lincoln and CaroMont Regional Medical Center were in network when we checked; Catawba Valley Medical Center was not.
  • Blue Value (10 plans, POS) is a narrower statewide Blue Cross network. Its out-of-network benefit is thin, so treat it as an in-network plan. Children's dental is included. Its standardized Silver costs $947 at age 45, $204 a month more than Ambetter's. CaroMont Regional Medical Center and Catawba Valley Medical Center were in network when we checked; Atrium Health Lincoln was not.
  • Blue Local with Atrium Health (10 plans, EPO) is built around Atrium Health's hospitals and doctors. It covers out-of-network care only in emergencies. Children's dental is included. Its standardized Silver costs $1,021 at age 45, $278 a month more than Ambetter's. Atrium Health Lincoln was in network when we checked; CaroMont Regional Medical Center and Catawba Valley Medical Center were not.
  • Blue Care (10 plans, HMO) is Blue Cross NC's statewide network. No referrals; out-of-network care is covered only in emergencies. Children's dental is included. Its standardized Silver costs $1,205 at age 45, $462 a month more than Ambetter's. Atrium Health Lincoln, CaroMont Regional Medical Center and Catawba Valley Medical Center were in network when we checked.
  • Blue Advantage (10 plans, PPO) is Blue Cross's nationwide option: it adds the national BlueCard network, so you are in network at Blue Cross doctors in every state. Its out-of-network benefit is thin, so treat it as a nationwide in-network plan. Children's dental is included. Its standardized Silver costs $1,241 at age 45, $498 a month more than Ambetter's. Atrium Health Lincoln, CaroMont Regional Medical Center and Catawba Valley Medical Center were in network when we checked.

Choose Ambetter if your doctors and hospital are in its network: the same benefits cost less there than anywhere else in the county. If you want to keep using Atrium Health Lincoln, look at Ambetter, Blue Local, Blue Care, Blue Advantage or Oscar. If you want to keep using CaroMont Regional Medical Center, look at Blue Value, Blue Care, Blue Advantage or Oscar. If you want to keep using Catawba Valley Medical Center, look at Blue Value, Blue Care or Blue Advantage. Choose Blue Advantage if you need in-network care outside North Carolina (months away each year, a student at an out-of-state college, frequent travel); it is the only nationwide network sold here.

Before enrolling, check each doctor and your hospital in the plan's own directory (Ambetter with Atrium Health, Blue Value, Blue Local with Atrium Health, Blue Care, Blue Advantage, Oscar with Atrium Health), or add them in our Covanu tool, which checks them against every plan's network. In Lincoln County, Atrium Health Lincoln in Lincolnton is the local hospital; residents also use CaroMont Regional Medical Center in Gastonia, Catawba Valley Medical Center in Hickory and, around Denver, the Lake Norman hospitals.

Decision 2: How much coverage do you expect to use?

Bronze, Silver and Gold describe how costs are split between you and the plan: roughly 60%, 70% and 80% paid by the plan for an average person. In Lincoln the practical differences are these:

  • Bronze (30 plans, $644 to $981 at 45): deductibles up to $9,350, so you pay for most care yourself until something serious happens, although two Bronze designs have no medical deductible (copays or coinsurance apply from the first dollar instead). All 30 Bronze plans are HSA-eligible for 2027 under the 2025 federal tax law, which lets you set aside up to $4,500 (single) or $9,000 (family) pre-tax, plus $1,000 if you are 55 or older.
  • Silver (25 plans, $727 to $1,260): the plans to look at first if you qualify for a subsidy, because incomes under about $39,900 (single) or $82,500 (family of four) unlock cost-sharing reductions that only apply to Silver plans. At $30,000 for one person the standardized Silver's deductible drops from $6,400 to $800 and its out-of-pocket maximum from $10,300 to $3,600. Without a cost-sharing reduction, Silver is a middle ground that often loses to either Bronze or Gold on total cost.
  • Gold (17 plans, $830 to $1,299): $0 to $2,500 deductibles and flat copays for most care. The cheapest Gold, Ambetter Signature Gold, costs $186 a month more than the cheapest Bronze at age 45, which is worth it if you take brand-name or specialty drugs, see specialists regularly, or are planning surgery or a pregnancy.

Decision 3: How are your prescriptions priced?

Each insurer has its own drug list, so check every prescription against the plans you are considering; our Covanu tool checks a list of medications against every plan in the county at once. (For Blue Cross, the finding from our other county guides holds here: its two drug lists covered the same 48 common drugs.) Beyond coverage, the plan design decides what you pay:

  • On the standardized plans, preferred brand drugs are flat copays with no deductible on Silver ($40) and Gold ($30), and specialty drugs are $350 after the deductible on Silver or $250 with no deductible on Gold.
  • Most other designs charge 25% to 50% coinsurance for non-preferred and specialty drugs, usually after a deductible, and several put brand drugs behind the deductible or a separate drug deductible. Someone on an expensive brand drug can pay thousands before the plan contributes.
  • Insulin and condition-specific plans. Oscar's Simple Diabetes and Chronic Care CKM plans, at the Bronze and Silver levels, cover endocrinologist visits and diabetic eye and foot exams in full and cap insulin at $100 for a 30-day supply (the CKM plans also make cardiology and pulmonology visits and common generic statins and inhalers free), its Breathe Easy plans make pulmonologist visits and common generic inhalers free, and its Women's Health Silver covers gynecologist visits and common generic hormone therapy in full. In the official coverage example for a year of managing type 2 diabetes, the estimated cost to you on the standardized Bronze is $4,720 with Ambetter, $4,620 with Blue Cross NC and $4,800 with Oscar: same deductible and copays, different drug lists and insulin terms. The example prices insulin at the plan's own rate, before any manufacturer program.
  • What insulin costs without a $0 benefit. It is priced like any brand-name drug: a preferred brand is a $40 copay with no deductible on the standardized Silver and $30 on the standardized Gold, while on most Bronze plans you pay the pharmacy price, about $66 to $72 for a vial of Humalog or NovoLog since the 2023–2024 list-price cuts, until you meet the deductible. Lilly, Novo Nordisk and Sanofi each cap their insulins at $35 a month for people with commercial coverage, which includes Marketplace plans, so a two-insulin routine usually costs $35 to $80 a month without a $0 benefit, about $400 to $1,000 a year. That is roughly what $0 insulin is worth to someone who uses those programs.

The arithmetic for brand and specialty drugs is in Bronze vs. Silver when you take medications. The short version: one brand-name prescription is often enough to make a standardized Silver cheaper than Bronze over a year, and a specialty drug makes a standardized Gold the cheapest plan on the shelf.

Frequently asked questions

Which insurers sell Marketplace plans in Lincoln County for 2027?

Three: Ambetter of North Carolina (15 plans), Blue Cross and Blue Shield of North Carolina (40 plans) and Oscar (17 plans), for 72 plans in all. Blue Cross sells each of its designs in Blue Value, Blue Local, Blue Care and Blue Advantage, and Ambetter and Oscar sell some designs both with and without adult dental and vision, so the 72 plans come down to 32 distinct designs.

Is Atrium Health Lincoln in network?

When we checked HealthCare.gov's doctor and hospital lookup on November 10, 2026, Atrium Health Lincoln was in network on every plan except Blue Value's; CaroMont Regional Medical Center was in network on every plan except Ambetter's and Blue Local's; Catawba Valley Medical Center was in network on Blue Value, Blue Care and Blue Advantage plans and not on Ambetter, Blue Local or Oscar plans. Networks change, and a hospital being in network does not mean every doctor who works there is, so check the specific doctors and facilities you use before you enroll.

Why do plans with the same benefits cost so differently after the subsidy?

Because the tax credit is a fixed dollar amount based on the second-cheapest Silver plan in your county, which in Lincoln is Ambetter Signature Silver ($743 a month at age 45). The credit makes that plan cost the same share of your income wherever you live; any plan that costs more at full price costs exactly that much more after the credit. That is why the standardized Silver, identical in benefits from every insurer, costs a 45-year-old earning $30,000 $154 a month from Ambetter and $652 from Blue Advantage.

What are the standardized "Easy Pricing" plans?

Plans whose deductibles, copays and coinsurance are set by the federal government, so a standardized plan at a given metal level has the same benefits whichever insurer sells it. HealthCare.gov labels them Easy Pricing. In Lincoln they are Ambetter's Signature plans, Blue Cross NC's Standard (Gold Standard A for Gold) plans and Oscar's Classic Standard plans. Because the benefits match, the comparison comes down to price, network and extras such as children's dental: at age 45 the standardized Silver ranges from $743 (Ambetter) to $1,241 (Blue Advantage).

Do I need a referral to see a specialist?

No. None of the plans sold here requires a referral from a primary care doctor to see a specialist, including the HMOs.

Which plans work outside North Carolina?

Only Blue Advantage, Blue Cross's PPO, gives you in-network access to doctors nationwide, through the BlueCard network. It costs about 3% more than the same Blue Care design: $27 to $40 a month at age 45, $50 to $75 at 60. The other plans are built around North Carolina providers. Every plan covers emergency care anywhere.

Is there a catastrophic plan in Lincoln County?

No. For 2027 no catastrophic plan is offered in Lincoln County, even for people under 30. The Bronze plans are the lowest-premium options, starting at $467 a month at age 27 before any subsidy.

Do the plans include dental and vision?

Children's dental is included in every Blue Value, Blue Local, Blue Care and Blue Advantage plan, but not in Ambetter and Oscar plans; families choosing those should add a stand-alone dental plan for the children. Included children's dental is worth about $250 to $700 a year per child, what a stand-alone children's dental plan costs in North Carolina for 2027 ($21 to $58 a month). It pays for checkups in full; on most plans, fillings and other treatment count toward the medical deductible, while a stand-alone plan has a $25 to $155 deductible and caps a child's costs at $350 to $450 a year, so a child who needs fillings can come out ahead with one. Adult dental and vision come only with the versions that say so, from Ambetter (about $19 to $26 a month more at age 45) and Oscar (about $22 to $23 a month more at age 45).

What happens if my income is under about $22,000?

North Carolina expanded Medicaid in December 2023. A single adult with income under roughly 138% of the federal poverty level (about $22,000 for one person, $29,900 for a couple, $45,500 for a family of four in 2027) is directed to NC Medicaid rather than receiving a Marketplace subsidy. The Marketplace application checks this automatically.

When can I enroll?

Open Enrollment for 2027 coverage runs November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027. Enroll by December 15 for coverage starting January 1. Outside Open Enrollment you need a qualifying event, such as losing other coverage, moving, marriage or a new baby.
