Health Insurance in New Hanover County, NC for 2027: All 75 Plans Explained

By Kurt Conger, independent licensed NC health insurance agent (NPN 21720013) · Updated November 10, 2026 · 22 minute read · Plan data: HealthCare.gov plan preview, 2027 plan year

If you buy your own health insurance in New Hanover County (Wilmington, Carolina Beach, Kure Beach, Wrightsville Beach, Ogden and Castle Hayne) you have four insurers and 75 Marketplace plans to choose from for 2027: Ambetter, Blue Cross and Blue Shield of North Carolina, UnitedHealthcare and Oscar. This guide uses the official HealthCare.gov plan data for New Hanover County to explain what the plans cost, how the insurers and their networks differ, and which plan fits which situation.

Key takeaways

  • 75 plans, four insurers, 38 designs. Ambetter sells 19 plans, Blue Cross NC 30 (10 designs in three networks), UnitedHealthcare 15 and Oscar 11. Many are one design sold twice, with and without adult dental and vision.
  • Same benefits, very different prices. All four insurers sell the federally standardized Bronze, Silver and Gold designs (marked Easy Pricing on HealthCare.gov). At age 45 Ambetter's standardized Silver costs $740 a month; the identical design costs $978 to $1,184 from the other insurers.
  • Your subsidy is set by Ambetter's prices. The benchmark plan is Ambetter's Clear Silver, so subsidized households pay little for Ambetter plans and a lot for Blue Cross: a 45-year-old earning $30,000 pays $547 a month for Blue Care Silver Standard here, against about $154 in Craven County, where Blue Cross is the only insurer.
  • Check the hospital first. Novant Health New Hanover Regional Medical Center was in network on every Ambetter, Blue Cross and UnitedHealthcare plan and on no Oscar plan when we checked HealthCare.gov on November 10, 2026.
  • Full-price premiums for a 45-year-old run $640 to $936 for Bronze, $726 to $1,202 for Silver and $816 to $1,349 for Gold. A 60-year-old pays about 1.9 times that; a 30-year-old about 0.8 times.
  • No referrals and no catastrophic plan. None of the plans we checked requires a referral to see a specialist, and no catastrophic plan is offered in New Hanover for 2027.

The New Hanover County market at a glance

New Hanover has four Marketplace insurers, against one in the 21 eastern counties our other guides cover, but the number of plans overstates how hard the choice is. The 75 plans are 38 designs, many of them sold more than once, and they come in six networks. That makes three decisions, in order: which insurer and network (start with your doctors and hospital), how much coverage, and how your prescriptions are priced. What subsidies do to those numbers

The premium tax credit is a fixed dollar amount based on your income and the price of the second-cheapest Silver plan, which in New Hanover is Ambetter's Clear Silver with $0 Insulin Options ($732 a month at age 45). You can spend the credit on any plan. Because the enhanced credits of 2021–2025 have expired, the credit stops entirely at 400% of the federal poverty level ($63,840 for one person, $86,560 for a couple, $132,000 for a family of four), and the Marketplace now recovers any overpaid credit in full at tax time, so your income estimate matters. Cells marked CSR include cost-sharing reductions, which also lower the deductible and out-of-pocket maximum. At $30,000 for one person, the deductible and out-of-pocket maximum drop to $800 and $3,600 on the standardized Silver plans (Ambetter Signature Silver and Blue Care Silver Standard) and to $850 and $3,700 on Streamlined Silver; at $35,000 they drop to $2,900 and $9,000, and to $3,450 and $8,500 on Streamlined Silver.

Three things to notice. First, the credit is the same dollar amount whichever plan you choose, so the full-price gaps between insurers pass straight through to what you pay: the two standardized Silver plans in the table have identical benefits, yet at $30,000 one costs $162 a month and the other $547. Second, no plan in the table is free. At age 45 the cheapest Bronze costs only $92 a month less than the benchmark, so the credit does not cover it and a 45-year-old earning $30,000 still pays $62; in Craven County, where the gap is about $300, the same person gets every Bronze plan for $0. Third, families should mind children's dental. Ambetter and Oscar plans leave it out, and because the benchmark is an Ambetter plan, the family's credit is a little larger to help pay for a stand-alone children's dental plan; Blue Cross and UnitedHealthcare plans already include it. And as everywhere in North Carolina, a single adult under about $22,000 or a couple under about $29,900 is sent to NC Medicaid instead of receiving a Marketplace credit; the application sorts this out.

Decision 1: Which insurer and network?

In the 21 eastern counties our other guides cover, this decision is between two versions of the same Blue Cross plan. In New Hanover it is the decision that matters most, because the networks really differ and the prices differ far more than the benefits do. Start with the doctors and the hospital you want to keep.

  • Ambetter (19 plans, HMO) has the lowest prices at every metal level, and New Hanover Regional is in network. As an HMO it covers out-of-network care only in emergencies, but no referrals are needed for specialists. Children's dental is not included.
  • Blue Cross NC (30 plans) sells the same 10 designs as in its other eastern counties, each in three networks. Blue Value is a narrower statewide network, sold as a POS plan and about 13% cheaper than Blue Care ($108 to $151 a month at age 45); its out-of-network benefit comes with a $35,000 deductible, so treat it as in-network only. Blue Care is the statewide HMO. Blue Advantage is a PPO that adds the national BlueCard network for about 5% more than Blue Care ($44 to $64 a month at 45, $83 to $120 at 60). All three include children's dental.
  • UnitedHealthcare (15 plans, HMO) includes $0 insulin on every plan and children's dental, and New Hanover Regional is in network. Its Bronze and Silver prices are close to Blue Care's; its Gold plans are the most expensive in the county.
  • Oscar (11 plans, HMO) is priced close to Blue Care, does not include children's dental, has HealthCare.gov's lowest quality rating in the county (2 stars), and did not have New Hanover Regional in network on any plan when we checked.

Choose Ambetter if your doctors are in its network: the same benefits cost less there than anywhere else in the county. Choose Blue Advantage if you need in-network care outside North Carolina (months away each year, a student at an out-of-state college, frequent travel); it is the only nationwide network sold here. Look at Blue Value if you want Blue Cross at a lower price and your doctors are in its narrower network, and at UnitedHealthcare if you use insulin.

Before enrolling, check each doctor and your hospital in the plan's own directory, or add them in HealthCare.gov's plan preview, which marks every plan as in or out of network for them. Blue Cross directories: Blue Care and Blue Advantage. In New Hanover County, Novant Health New Hanover Regional Medical Center in Wilmington is the hospital most residents use, and Novant's Scotts Hill Medical Center, a satellite of New Hanover Regional, opened north of town in June 2026; check the one you would use.

Decision 2: How much coverage do you expect to use?

Bronze, Silver and Gold describe how costs are split between you and the plan: roughly 60%, 70% and 80% paid by the plan for an average person. In New Hanover the practical differences are these:

  • Bronze (28 plans, $640 to $936 at 45): deductibles up to $11,750, so you pay for most care yourself until something serious happens, although three Bronze designs have no medical deductible (copays or coinsurance apply from the first dollar instead). All 28 Bronze plans are HSA-eligible for 2027 under the 2025 federal tax law, which lets you set aside up to $4,500 (single) or $9,000 (family) pre-tax, plus $1,000 if you are 55 or older.
  • Silver (28 plans, $726 to $1,202): the plans to look at first if you qualify for a subsidy, because incomes under about $39,900 (single) or $82,500 (family of four) unlock cost-sharing reductions that only apply to Silver plans. At $30,000 for one person the standardized Silver's deductible drops from $6,400 to $800 and its out-of-pocket maximum from $10,300 to $3,600. Without a cost-sharing reduction, Silver is a middle ground that often loses to either Bronze or Gold on total cost.
  • Gold (19 plans, $816 to $1,349): $0 to $2,500 deductibles and flat copays for most care. Gold is unusually affordable here if you buy it from Ambetter: Signature Gold costs $176 a month more than the cheapest Bronze at age 45, and less than every Bronze plan from Blue Care, Blue Advantage, UnitedHealthcare and Oscar. It is worth a look if you take brand-name or specialty drugs, see specialists regularly, or are planning surgery or a pregnancy.

Decision 3: How are your prescriptions priced?

Each insurer has its own drug list, so check every prescription against the plans you are considering; our Covanu tool checks a list of medications against all 75 plans at once. (For Blue Cross, the finding from our other county guides holds here: its two drug lists covered the same 48 common drugs.) Beyond coverage, the plan design decides what you pay:

  • On the standardized plans (Ambetter Signature, Blue Cross Standard and Gold Standard A, UnitedHealthcare Standard, Oscar Classic Standard), preferred brand drugs are flat copays with no deductible on Silver ($40) and Gold ($30), and specialty drugs are $350 after the deductible on Silver or $250 with no deductible on Gold.
  • Most other designs, from every insurer, charge 25% to 50% coinsurance for non-preferred and specialty drugs, usually after a deductible, and several put brand drugs behind the deductible or a separate drug deductible. Someone on an expensive brand drug can pay thousands before the plan contributes.
  • Insulin and condition-specific plans. UnitedHealthcare includes $0 insulin on every plan; Ambetter's Clear Silver has $0 insulin options, and its Enhanced CHF Heart Care and Asthma/COPD Silver plans have $0 options for related prescriptions; Oscar has Simple Diabetes plans at the Bronze and Silver levels. In the official coverage example for a year of managing type 2 diabetes, the estimated cost to you on UnitedHealthcare's standardized Bronze is $700, against $4,720 for Ambetter's and $4,800 for Oscar's version of the same design.

The arithmetic for brand and specialty drugs is in Bronze vs. Silver when you take medications. The short version: one brand-name prescription is often enough to make a standardized Silver cheaper than Bronze over a year, and a specialty drug makes a standardized Gold the cheapest plan on the shelf.

Frequently asked questions

Which insurers sell Marketplace plans in New Hanover County for 2027?

Four: Ambetter of North Carolina (19 plans), Blue Cross and Blue Shield of North Carolina (30), UnitedHealthcare (15) and Oscar (11), for 75 plans in all. Blue Cross sells each of its 10 designs in three networks (Blue Value, Blue Care and Blue Advantage), and Ambetter, UnitedHealthcare and Oscar sell several designs both with and without adult dental and vision, so the 75 plans come down to 38 distinct designs.

Is Novant Health New Hanover Regional Medical Center in network?

When we checked HealthCare.gov's doctor and hospital lookup on November 10, 2026, New Hanover Regional Medical Center was listed as in network on all 19 Ambetter plans, all 30 Blue Cross plans and all 15 UnitedHealthcare plans, and as out of network on all 11 Oscar plans. Networks change, and a hospital being in network does not mean every doctor who works there is, so check the specific doctors and facilities you use before you enroll.

Why do Blue Cross plans cost so much more after the subsidy here than in Craven or Carteret County?

Because the tax credit is based on the second-cheapest Silver plan in your county, and it is designed to make that benchmark plan cost the same share of your income wherever you live. A 45-year-old earning $30,000 pays about $154 a month for the benchmark both here and in Craven County. In Craven the benchmark is Blue Care Silver Standard; in New Hanover it is Ambetter's Clear Silver, which costs $732 at full price against $1,125 for Blue Care Silver Standard. Choosing Blue Cross here means paying that full price difference yourself, which is why Blue Care Silver Standard costs that same person $547 a month.

What are the standardized "Easy Pricing" plans?

Plans whose deductibles, copays and coinsurance are set by the federal government, so a standardized plan at a given metal level has the same benefits whichever insurer sells it. HealthCare.gov labels them Easy Pricing. In New Hanover they are Ambetter's Signature plans, Blue Cross's Standard plans (Gold Standard A for Gold), UnitedHealthcare's Standard plans and Oscar's Classic Standard plans. Because the benefits match, the comparison comes down to price, network and extras such as children's dental: at age 45 the standardized Silver ranges from $740 (Ambetter) to $1,184 (Blue Advantage).

Do I need a referral to see a specialist?

No. None of the plans we checked requires a referral from a primary care doctor to see a specialist, including the HMOs.

Which plans work outside North Carolina?

Only Blue Advantage, Blue Cross's PPO, gives you in-network access to doctors nationwide, through the BlueCard network. It costs about 5% more than the same Blue Care design: $44 to $64 a month at age 45, $83 to $120 at 60. The Ambetter, Blue Care, UnitedHealthcare and Oscar plans are HMOs built around North Carolina providers, and Blue Value's out-of-network benefit comes with a $35,000 deductible. Every plan covers emergency care anywhere.

Is there a catastrophic plan in New Hanover County?

No. For 2027 no catastrophic plan is offered in New Hanover County, even for people under 30. The Bronze plans are the lowest-premium options, starting at $465 a month at age 27 before any subsidy.

Do the plans include dental and vision?

Children's dental is included in every Blue Cross and UnitedHealthcare plan, but not in Ambetter or Oscar plans; families choosing those should add a stand-alone dental plan for the children. Adult dental and vision come only with the versions that say so: Ambetter's "+ Vision + Adult Dental" plans (about $19 to $26 a month more at age 45), UnitedHealthcare's "+" plans (about $24 more) and two Oscar plans "with Adult Dental & Vision" (about $29 more).

What happens if my income is under about $22,000?

North Carolina expanded Medicaid in December 2023. A single adult with income under roughly 138% of the federal poverty level (about $22,000 for one person, $29,900 for a couple, $45,500 for a family of four in 2027) is directed to NC Medicaid rather than receiving a Marketplace subsidy. The Marketplace application checks this automatically.

When can I enroll?

Open Enrollment for 2027 coverage runs November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027 on HealthCare.gov. Enroll by December 15 for coverage starting January 1. Outside Open Enrollment you need a qualifying event, such as losing other coverage, moving, marriage or a new baby.
