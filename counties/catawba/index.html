Health Insurance in Catawba County, NC for 2027: All 72 Plans Explained

By Kurt Conger, independent licensed NC health insurance agent (NPN 21720013) · Updated November 10, 2026 · 22 minute read · Plan data: HealthCare.gov Marketplace API, 2027 plan year

If you buy your own health insurance in Catawba County (Hickory, Newton, Conover, Maiden, Claremont and Sherrills Ford) you have four insurers and 72 Marketplace plans to choose from for 2027: Ambetter of North Carolina, AmeriHealth Caritas Next, Blue Cross and Blue Shield of North Carolina and UnitedHealthcare. This guide uses the official HealthCare.gov plan data for Catawba County to explain what the plans cost, how the insurers and their networks differ, and which plan fits which situation.

Key takeaways

  • 72 plans, four insurers, 37 designs. Ambetter 19, AmeriHealth Caritas Next 8, Blue Cross NC 30 (10 designs in three networks) and UnitedHealthcare 15. Many are one design sold twice, with and without adult dental and vision.
  • Same benefits, very different prices. Every network sells the federally standardized Bronze, Silver and Gold designs (marked Easy Pricing on HealthCare.gov). At age 45 Ambetter's standardized Silver costs $679 a month; the identical design costs up to $1,172 from Blue Advantage.
  • Your subsidy is set by Ambetter Clear Silver with $0 Insulin Options, the second-cheapest Silver plan ($671 at full price, age 45). The credit is the same dollar amount whatever you buy, so a 45-year-old earning $30,000 pays $154 for that plan and $655 for Blue Advantage's standardized Silver.
  • Check the hospital first. In HealthCare.gov's lookup on November 10, 2026, Catawba Valley Medical Center was in network on every plan except Ambetter's and UnitedHealthcare's; Frye Regional Medical Center was in network on every plan.
  • Full-price premiums for a 45-year-old run $587 to $950 for Bronze, $666 to $1,190 for Silver and $749 to $1,370 for Gold. A 60-year-old pays about 1.9 times that; a 30-year-old about 0.8 times.
  • No referrals and no catastrophic plan. None of the plans requires a referral to see a specialist, and no catastrophic plan is offered in Catawba for 2027.

The Catawba County market at a glance

Catawba has four Marketplace insurers, but the number of plans overstates how hard the choice is. The 72 plans are 37 designs, many of them sold more than once, and they come in six networks. That makes three decisions, in order: which insurer and network (start with your doctors and hospital), how much coverage, and how your prescriptions are priced. What subsidies do to those numbers

The premium tax credit is a fixed dollar amount based on your income and the price of the second-cheapest Silver plan, which in Catawba is Ambetter Clear Silver with $0 Insulin Options ($671 a month at age 45). You can spend the credit on any plan. Because the enhanced credits of 2021–2025 have expired, the credit stops entirely at 400% of the federal poverty level ($63,840 for one person, $86,560 for a couple, $132,000 for a family of four), and the Marketplace now recovers any overpaid credit in full at tax time, so your income estimate matters. Three things to notice. First, the credit is the same dollar amount whichever plan you choose, so the full-price gaps between insurers pass straight through to what you pay: the standardized Silver has identical benefits everywhere, yet at $30,000 it costs $162 a month from Ambetter and $655 from Blue Advantage. Second, no plan here is free at $30,000. At age 45 the cheapest Bronze costs only $84 a month less than the benchmark, so the credit does not cover it and a 45-year-old earning $30,000 still pays $70. Third, families should mind children's dental: Ambetter and AmeriHealth Caritas Next plans leave it out, while Blue Value, Blue Care, Blue Advantage and UnitedHealthcare plans include it. And as everywhere in North Carolina, a single adult under about $22,000 or a couple under about $29,900 is sent to NC Medicaid instead of receiving a Marketplace credit; the application sorts this out.

Decision 1: Which insurer and network?

Where Blue Cross is the only insurer, this decision is between two versions of the same plan. In Catawba it is the decision that matters most, because the networks really differ and the prices differ far more than the benefits do. Start with the doctors and the hospital you want to keep.

  • Ambetter (19 plans, HMO) is Ambetter of North Carolina's network. As an HMO it covers out-of-network care only in emergencies, but no referrals are needed for specialists. Children's dental is not included. Its standardized plans are the cheapest in the county ($679 for Silver at age 45). Frye Regional Medical Center was in network when we checked; Catawba Valley Medical Center was not.
  • AmeriHealth Caritas Next (8 plans, HMO) is AmeriHealth Caritas Next's network, with free 24/7 virtual primary and urgent care through its telehealth provider and no referrals. Out-of-network care is covered only in emergencies, and children's dental is not included. Its standardized Silver costs $821 at age 45, $142 a month more than Ambetter's. Catawba Valley Medical Center and Frye Regional Medical Center were in network when we checked.
  • Blue Value (10 plans, POS) is a narrower statewide Blue Cross network. Its out-of-network benefit is thin, so treat it as an in-network plan. Children's dental is included. Its standardized Silver costs $969 at age 45, $290 a month more than Ambetter's. Catawba Valley Medical Center and Frye Regional Medical Center were in network when we checked.
  • Blue Care (10 plans, HMO) is Blue Cross NC's statewide network. No referrals; out-of-network care is covered only in emergencies. Children's dental is included. Its standardized Silver costs $1,139 at age 45, $460 a month more than Ambetter's. Catawba Valley Medical Center and Frye Regional Medical Center were in network when we checked.
  • UnitedHealthcare (15 plans, HMO) is UnitedHealthcare's network, with $0 insulin on every plan and children's dental included. Out-of-network care is covered only in emergencies. Its standardized Silver costs $1,141 at age 45, $462 a month more than Ambetter's. Frye Regional Medical Center was in network when we checked; Catawba Valley Medical Center was not.
  • Blue Advantage (10 plans, PPO) is Blue Cross's nationwide option: it adds the national BlueCard network, so you are in network at Blue Cross doctors in every state. Its out-of-network benefit is thin, so treat it as a nationwide in-network plan. Children's dental is included. Its standardized Silver costs $1,172 at age 45, $493 a month more than Ambetter's. Catawba Valley Medical Center and Frye Regional Medical Center were in network when we checked.

Choose Ambetter if your doctors and hospital are in its network: the same benefits cost less there than anywhere else in the county. If you want to keep using Catawba Valley Medical Center, look at AmeriHealth Caritas Next, Blue Value, Blue Care or Blue Advantage. Choose Blue Advantage if you need in-network care outside North Carolina (months away each year, a student at an out-of-state college, frequent travel); it is the only nationwide network sold here. Look at UnitedHealthcare if you use insulin.

Before enrolling, check each doctor and your hospital in the plan's own directory (Ambetter, AmeriHealth Caritas Next, Blue Value, Blue Care, Blue Advantage, UnitedHealthcare), or add them in HealthCare.gov's plan preview, which marks every plan as in or out of network for them. In Catawba County, Catawba Valley Medical Center and Frye Regional Medical Center, both in Hickory, are the local hospitals.

Decision 2: How much coverage do you expect to use?

Bronze, Silver and Gold describe how costs are split between you and the plan: roughly 60%, 70% and 80% paid by the plan for an average person. In Catawba the practical differences are these:

  • Bronze (26 plans, $587 to $950 at 45): deductibles up to $12,000, so you pay for most care yourself until something serious happens, although two Bronze designs have no medical deductible (copays or coinsurance apply from the first dollar instead). All 26 Bronze plans are HSA-eligible for 2027 under the 2025 federal tax law, which lets you set aside up to $4,500 (single) or $9,000 (family) pre-tax, plus $1,000 if you are 55 or older.
  • Silver (25 plans, $666 to $1,190): the plans to look at first if you qualify for a subsidy, because incomes under about $39,900 (single) or $82,500 (family of four) unlock cost-sharing reductions that only apply to Silver plans. At $30,000 for one person the standardized Silver's deductible drops from $6,400 to $800 and its out-of-pocket maximum from $10,300 to $3,600. Without a cost-sharing reduction, Silver is a middle ground that often loses to either Bronze or Gold on total cost.
  • Gold (21 plans, $749 to $1,370): $0 to $2,500 deductibles and flat copays for most care. The cheapest Gold, Ambetter Signature Gold, costs $162 a month more than the cheapest Bronze at age 45, which is worth it if you take brand-name or specialty drugs, see specialists regularly, or are planning surgery or a pregnancy.

Decision 3: How are your prescriptions priced?

Each insurer has its own drug list, so check every prescription against the plans you are considering; our Covanu tool checks a list of medications against every plan in the county at once. (For Blue Cross, the finding from our other county guides holds here: its two drug lists covered the same 48 common drugs.) Beyond coverage, the plan design decides what you pay:

  • On the standardized plans, preferred brand drugs are flat copays with no deductible on Silver ($40) and Gold ($30), and specialty drugs are $350 after the deductible on Silver or $250 with no deductible on Gold.
  • Most other designs charge 25% to 50% coinsurance for non-preferred and specialty drugs, usually after a deductible, and several put brand drugs behind the deductible or a separate drug deductible. Someone on an expensive brand drug can pay thousands before the plan contributes.
  • Insulin and condition-specific plans. UnitedHealthcare includes $0 insulin on every plan; Ambetter's Clear Silver has $0 insulin options, and its Enhanced CHF Heart Care and Asthma/COPD Silver plans have $0 options for related prescriptions. In the official coverage example for a year of managing type 2 diabetes, the estimated cost to you on the standardized Bronze is $4,720 with Ambetter, $4,520 with AmeriHealth Caritas Next, $4,620 with Blue Cross NC and $700 with UnitedHealthcare: same deductible and copays, different drug lists and insulin terms.

The arithmetic for brand and specialty drugs is in Bronze vs. Silver when you take medications. The short version: one brand-name prescription is often enough to make a standardized Silver cheaper than Bronze over a year, and a specialty drug makes a standardized Gold the cheapest plan on the shelf.

Frequently asked questions

Which insurers sell Marketplace plans in Catawba County for 2027?

Four: Ambetter of North Carolina (19 plans), AmeriHealth Caritas Next (8 plans), Blue Cross and Blue Shield of North Carolina (30 plans) and UnitedHealthcare (15 plans), for 72 plans in all. Blue Cross sells each of its designs in Blue Value, Blue Care and Blue Advantage, and Ambetter and UnitedHealthcare sell some designs both with and without adult dental and vision, so the 72 plans come down to 37 distinct designs.

Is Catawba Valley Medical Center in network?

When we checked HealthCare.gov's doctor and hospital lookup on November 10, 2026, Catawba Valley Medical Center was in network on every plan except Ambetter's and UnitedHealthcare's; Frye Regional Medical Center was in network on every plan. Networks change, and a hospital being in network does not mean every doctor who works there is, so check the specific doctors and facilities you use before you enroll.

Why do plans with the same benefits cost so differently after the subsidy?

Because the tax credit is a fixed dollar amount based on the second-cheapest Silver plan in your county, which in Catawba is Ambetter Clear Silver with $0 Insulin Options ($671 a month at age 45). The credit makes that plan cost the same share of your income wherever you live; any plan that costs more at full price costs exactly that much more after the credit. That is why the standardized Silver, identical in benefits from every insurer, costs a 45-year-old earning $30,000 $162 a month from Ambetter and $655 from Blue Advantage.

What are the standardized "Easy Pricing" plans?

Plans whose deductibles, copays and coinsurance are set by the federal government, so a standardized plan at a given metal level has the same benefits whichever insurer sells it. HealthCare.gov labels them Easy Pricing. In Catawba they are Ambetter's Signature plans, AmeriHealth Caritas Next's Signature plans, Blue Cross NC's Standard (Gold Standard A for Gold) plans and UnitedHealthcare's Standard plans. Because the benefits match, the comparison comes down to price, network and extras such as children's dental: at age 45 the standardized Silver ranges from $679 (Ambetter) to $1,172 (Blue Advantage).

Do I need a referral to see a specialist?

No. None of the plans sold here requires a referral from a primary care doctor to see a specialist, including the HMOs.

Which plans work outside North Carolina?

Only Blue Advantage, Blue Cross's PPO, gives you in-network access to doctors nationwide, through the BlueCard network. It costs about 3% more than the same Blue Care design: $26 to $38 a month at age 45, $47 to $71 at 60. The other plans are built around North Carolina providers. Every plan covers emergency care anywhere.

Is there a catastrophic plan in Catawba County?

No. For 2027 no catastrophic plan is offered in Catawba County, even for people under 30. The Bronze plans are the lowest-premium options, starting at $426 a month at age 27 before any subsidy.

Do the plans include dental and vision?

Children's dental is included in every Blue Value, Blue Care, Blue Advantage and UnitedHealthcare plan, but not in Ambetter and AmeriHealth Caritas Next plans; families choosing those should add a stand-alone dental plan for the children. Adult dental and vision come only with the versions that say so, from Ambetter (about $18 to $24 a month more at age 45) and UnitedHealthcare (about $24 to $25 a month more at age 45).

What happens if my income is under about $22,000?

North Carolina expanded Medicaid in December 2023. A single adult with income under roughly 138% of the federal poverty level (about $22,000 for one person, $29,900 for a couple, $45,500 for a family of four in 2027) is directed to NC Medicaid rather than receiving a Marketplace subsidy. The Marketplace application checks this automatically.

When can I enroll?

Open Enrollment for 2027 coverage runs November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027 on HealthCare.gov. Enroll by December 15 for coverage starting January 1. Outside Open Enrollment you need a qualifying event, such as losing other coverage, moving, marriage or a new baby.
