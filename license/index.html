What a North Carolina health insurance license is

The North Carolina Department of Insurance licenses insurance agents as insurance producers. Each license lists the kinds of insurance the agent may sell, called lines of authority, and state law forbids selling, soliciting or negotiating any kind of insurance without a license for that line.

Kurt’s license carries the Accident & Health or Sickness line, North Carolina’s health insurance license. State law defines it as insurance coverage for sickness, bodily injury or accidental death, which may include disability income benefits. It is the license an agent needs to sell health insurance in North Carolina, including the ACA Marketplace plans covered on this site.

To earn it, an applicant must be at least 18, pass the state licensing exam for that line of authority (North Carolina’s exams are given by Pearson VUE), complete the background check described below, and apply to the Department of Insurance, which decides whether to issue the license.

To keep it, a North Carolina agent must complete 24 hours of approved continuing education every two years, including 3 hours of ethics. If an agent misses the deadline, the license expires. As the certificate says, the license stays valid until it is cancelled, surrendered or revoked.

The license includes a criminal background check

Every first-time applicant for a North Carolina resident insurance producer license must be fingerprinted for a state and federal criminal history check, as required by North Carolina General Statute 58-33-48. The Department of Insurance reviews the criminal history record, along with the applicant’s answers to the application’s background questions, before it decides whether to issue the license.

Kurt completed this background check before North Carolina issued his license on September 19, 2025.
